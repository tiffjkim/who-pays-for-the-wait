4. Is the affordable wait really longer?

Not necessarily on average. That is the interesting part.

What the research says

The Atlanta Fed finds little difference in development time between publicly and privately funded multifamily projects. Planning and permitting run 3–4 months longer than construction.

What it can't tell us

An average hides the spread. The summary I could read does not say how much individual projects vary, or what happens to the project that misses a round.

Our hypothesis

The gap isn't the average wait. It's whether you know the answer before you commit. A date you can't predict is a cost nobody put in the pro forma.

The hypothesis is ours and not a finding of the paper. Also reported: durations are longer in the Northeast and West, and in mixed-use and conversion projects.

Hypothesis: where the cost goes when the wall holds. Back to the public line. A deal stalls when its last absorber is spent: market-rate when returns fall below what equity will accept, affordable when the subsidy line cannot grow. In a tight market, market-rate deals lose their cushion and start looking for public money too.
