Terner Center, California tax credit projects: 699 awarded applications, 2020–2023. These are associations after controlling for location, building type and size, not proof of cause. The authors say fragmentation is not the only or largest cost driver.

What it shows

In affordable housing, the wait is partly a count: how many funders have to line up, and how many rounds it takes.

The catch: survivors only

Terner looks at projects that won an award. Studies of completed multifamily projects, like the Atlanta Fed's, find little difference between public and private funding, but they can't count projects that stalled or died.

Our hypothesis

The real gap isn't the average wait. It's how many projects never finish, and whether you know the answer before you commit.

The hypothesis is ours, not a finding of either study. Nobody here has a count of affordable projects that never got built. That is a question for the room.

Questions for the room

  1. Think of the last deal you saw stall. Who paid for the wait, and was it on anyone's pro forma?
  2. What was that deal's last absorber: returns, subsidy, sponsor fee, or nobody?

Sources

  1. Massachusetts, Home for Everyone construction finance explainer: two or more cycles for 9% credits; each added source about 1.7% per unit; capitalized interest about 4% of cost; delays covered by added subsidy or donations; state-funded new construction above $650,000 per unit in 2024.
  2. Massachusetts winter 2026 rental round notice: notice, pre-application, full application and estimated award timing (about nine months).
  3. Goulston & Storrs, Boston large project review timeline: median 10 months over a decade; tail of three to four years.
  4. Terner Center, Reducing the complexity in California's affordable housing finance system (April 2025): 699 awarded tax credit applications, 2020–2023; about four months and $20,460 per unit per added public source; 27% reapplied. Associations, not proven causes.
  5. Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta, How long does it take to build multifamily housing?: little difference in development time between public and private funding. Read it alongside its limits: it covers built projects.
  6. HUD, What Happens to Low-Income Housing Tax Credit Properties: rent limits tied to area median income.
  7. City of Boston, example city-land RFP: shows the issue-to-response window only. Selection and conveyance timing are not published, so that step is left out of the chart.
